# Turn clean clips into paid, permitted distribution

The upgrade that matters most for "The Best Part" is the business model, not the editor. MrBeast-style entertainment is the **lowest-paying clipping category (~$1.00 per 1K views)**. YouTube Shorts pays roughly **$0.01–0.07 per 1K** and excludes unedited reuploads. TikTok Creator Rewards is not available in Pakistan, and Facebook payouts require bank and tax details from another country. So the money comes from three places: **permission-based campaigns (Vyro, Whop Content Rewards)**, a second account in a higher-paying niche, and **selling clipping as a service ($500–1,500/month per client)**. On tooling, our hand-picked, cold-open pipeline already beats OpusClip on its two most criticized failures: lost context and cut punchlines. We should copy OpusClip's *rubric*, *hook generator* and *reframing rules*, not its virality score, AI B-roll or scheduler. Borrowing scoring and crop logic from three open-source repos takes two installs (faster-whisper and PySceneDetect + MediaPipe). Publishing needs one master MP4, per-platform copy files and the free native schedulers. Self-built YouTube and TikTok API uploads stay private until an app audit passes. The build list and 30/60/90-day plan at the end turn this into work orders.

## OpusClip sells a rubric; the score itself is unreliable

OpusClip's Virality Score (0–99) grades each candidate on four qualitative axes, **Hook, Flow, Value and Trend**, conditioned on a detected genre. It publishes no weights and no validation ([OpusClip help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score.md)). ClipAnything adds visual objects and actions, non-speech audio events (laughter, cheering), and sentiment such as surprise or argument ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/clipanything)). In "Hands-Free" mode it applies genre-specific prompt presets. Users say this is where it breaks: the AI "misses the point", **cuts jokes before the punchline**, drops context sentences, and low-scored clips sometimes beat the "winners". One G2 reviewer kept about 40% of output ([eesel review roundup](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/opusclip-reviews)). The lesson is to copy the rubric as an LLM prompt, keep the human final pick, and enforce a rule Opus lacks: **the clip must contain its setup sentence and end after the reaction**.

OpusClip's own analysis of 13.5M clips (vendor data, correlational) is still useful as a style check. **Outcome-first hooks drew about 2× the views of the worst hook type, and story-setup openers ranked last.** About 80% of viral-tier clips had burned-in captions. Viral-tier median length was **41 s versus 50 s** overall. Mixing niche and broad hashtags got 2.5× the median views of niche-only tags ([OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)). Our cold-open-on-payoff structure is already an outcome-first hook. The actionable change is to aim for **30–45 s** instead of filling the 60 s ceiling.

Across OpusClip, Submagic, Captions, Vizard and Klap, the paid feature set has converged. Every one offers animated word captions with keyword and emoji highlights, an auto "headline" hook overlay, filler and silence removal, B-roll, brand templates and translation ([OpusClip brand template](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/how-to-add-a-brand-template.md); [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/features/magic-clips); [Captions](https://www.captions.ai/)). OpusClip's reframing is simple whitelist logic. It detects faces and screenshare, then picks Fill, Fit, Split (two speakers in the same frame), Three/Four, Screenshare or Gameplay, among only the layouts the user enabled ([OpusClip layouts](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/layout-and-reframing.md)). Its scheduler is Pro-only ($29/month) and is reported to drop connections and fail silently, worst on TikTok ([frankx.ai](https://frankx.ai/blog/ultimate-opus-clip-workflow-2026)).

| Copy (worth it for our genre) | Skip (gimmick or redundant) |
|---|---|
| Hook/Flow/Value/Trend rubric with payoff + emotion fields | Numeric virality score as the decider |
| Genre prompt presets (stunt: reveal, fail, money, last-to-leave) | AI-generated B-roll (MrBeast footage is already visual) |
| Auto headline: 5 outcome-first hook variants, ≤6 words, yellow keyword | Eye-contact fix |
| Layout rules: steady face crop, Split only for 2 speakers | Auto-scheduler (fragile, and posting needs approval anyway) |
| Filler/silence tightening with a review list | Translation, until a multi-language channel exists |
| Proper-noun vocabulary list for ASR | |

## Three repos are worth mining, but none is worth adopting whole

No open-source repo beats a hand-tuned pipeline on quality, but three hold ideas worth lifting. **Clips Kitty** (AGPL-3.0, native Windows) builds five per-second signals: audio, visual, reaction, hook and payoff. It normalizes them within each video, adds a capped bonus from YouTube "most replayed" data, scores candidates 0–100 with an LLM, and then **reranks the finalists head-to-head in one call**, because relative comparison works better than absolute scoring. Its crop follows the active speaker using TalkNet, and **a speaker change triggers a cut rather than a pan** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ColinGPT9/clips-studio)). **AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator** (MIT, 6.2k stars) splits sources longer than 30 min into 20-min chunks with 60 s overlap. It dedups candidates that overlap by more than 50% and ships an editable eight-signal rubric (hooks, emotional peaks, revelations, conflict, payoff and more) with JSON output fields we can reuse ([highlights.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator/main/shorts_generator/highlights.py)). **OpenShorts** (MIT, 8.8k stars) assigns each scene one of four layouts: TRACK, GENERAL (blurred background), SPLIT and SCREENCAST. It also accepts any OpenAI-compatible LLM endpoint ([GitHub](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts)). Copy ideas, not code, because the AGPL repos would infect anything we distribute.

Our `candidates.py` already does a lexicon-plus-loudness first pass. The gap is the second stage: an LLM rubric score, the overlap dedup and a head-to-head rerank of the top ten. That is prompt work, not installs. For the building blocks, every active repo uses **faster-whisper large-v3-turbo with `word_timestamps=True`**. Its batched pipeline cut a large-v2 run from 63 s to 17 s for about 1.5 GB of extra VRAM ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). Today we time words from YouTube auto-captions (json3). Those are free and fast, but they fail on sources with no captions and on client footage, so faster-whisper is the fallback we need. **PySceneDetect** (BSD-3, released Nov 2026) supplies scene cuts ([GitHub](https://github.com/Breakthrough/PySceneDetect)). **MediaPipe** (Apache-2.0) supplies face boxes without YOLO's AGPL license ([GitHub](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe)). Put together, these give a steady crop per scene with a blurred-background fallback. That is a real quality step over our fixed 8:9 panel when the subject sits off-centre. Full active-speaker detection (Light-ASD at 94% mAP, but research-grade scripts) is overkill for single-presenter MrBeast shots. It belongs only in a podcast-client workflow ([Light-ASD](https://github.com/Junhua-Liao/Light-ASD)). **auto-editor** (Unlicense) handles silence tightening with flags such as `--edit audio:threshold=0.04 --margin 0.2sec` ([GitHub](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor)). Skip pycaps (it duplicates HyperFrames captions), stable-ts (archived) and the faceless generators ShortGPT and MoneyPrinterTurbo.

The RTX 5080 (sm_120) brings one trap. Install torch from the **cu128-or-newer index**, never from default PyPI, and confirm that `torch.cuda.get_arch_list()` includes `sm_120` ([PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/pytorch-support-for-sm120/216099?page=3)). faster-whisper on Windows also needs the CUDA 12 cuBLAS and cuDNN 9 DLLs on PATH. WhisperX pins Python below 3.14, so it needs a separate 3.12 venv ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/whisperx/json)). No published benchmark compares faster-whisper with whisper.cpp on this GPU, so measure both before switching.

## One master file fits all six platforms; the copy and covers differ

A single export works almost everywhere: **1080×1920, H.264 High, AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo, 30 fps CFR, closed GOP of 2–5 s, `-movflags +faststart`, ≤60 s and under 100 MB**. That matches our current recipe, plus a size guard and faststart. The binding limits are the Instagram API (5–90 s for the Reels tab, 100 MB), Facebook Reels (**3–90 s**, closed GOP, AAC ≥128 kbps) ([Meta Reels API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/video-api/guides/reels-publishing)) and Snapchat Spotlight. Snapchat sources conflict (60 s vs 3 min), and 10–30 s is the reported sweet spot ([Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/specs/snapchat-spotlight-video-length-limit)). YouTube Shorts accepts up to 3 minutes ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/)). Since 25 July 2026, YouTube Partner Program (YPP) channels can set **custom Shorts thumbnails in desktop Studio** ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/24/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails-neal-mohan-feature-update/)). Instagram caps posts at **5 hashtags** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-implementing-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)), and Snapchat reportedly allows 160 characters with up to 5 tags. Our 9:16 cover maps directly to Instagram's `cover_url` and the YouTube Shorts thumbnail.

| Platform | Copy to generate | Publish via |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Title ≤100 chars, description + credit, 3–4 tags, custom cover (YPP) | YouTube Studio scheduler |
| Instagram Reels | Hook line, 5 hashtags, alt text, cover, Trial Reel | Meta Business Suite / app |
| Facebook Reels | Instagram caption reused, Page only | Meta Business Suite |
| TikTok | Caption ≤2,200 + hashtags | TikTok web scheduler |
| X | ≤280 chars, hook + credit | Manual |
| Snapchat Spotlight | ≤160 chars, ≤5 tags; prefer a 10–30 s cut | Manual (no public API) |

Cross-posting is safe if each upload is a clean master. Instagram keeps Reels that carry TikTok watermarks out of recommendations ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-now-limit-the-reach-of-re-posts-from-tiktok-within-its-reels/594803)). It also stops recommending accounts that mostly post unoriginal content, and a credit or border alone does not count as creative input ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy)). Our own logo carries no penalty ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)). Full API automation is a trap for a solo creator. Uploads from an unaudited YouTube API project are **locked private and can't be appealed** ([Google docs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos)). Unaudited TikTok clients can only post SELF_ONLY ([TikTok docs](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-reference-direct-post)). Meta's API is open: 100 Instagram posts and 30 Facebook Reels per 24 h, Trial Reels included ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing)). Automate the export and the copy files, and publish through the native schedulers. Consider Postiz Cloud, whose platform apps are already approved, only if volume outgrows manual posting.

## Campaigns pay first, but services pay sustainably

The paid clipping market is real but thin per view. A snapshot of **707 live Whop Content Rewards campaigns** (10 Nov 2026) showed a **median listed rate of $1.09 per 1K views**. YouTube had the highest platform median at $1.425, falling to $1.10 by 16 Nov, and only eight campaigns paid for Facebook. Category medians run **Tech and Health $2.00, Finance and Sports $1.50, Gaming $1.41, Entertainment $1.00**, and the vendor itself warns that "a $1.00 CPM is not a $1.00 earning" ([Content Rewards](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-cpm-rates-november-2026)). Whop has no follower gate, takes 10% of CPM payouts, and requires a bank account in the creator's registered-country currency. **No source confirms that Pakistan payouts work**, so a small test withdrawal is step one ([Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/payout-methods)). Vyro, MrBeast's program, requires **5,000 views per clip** and had a median of about $2.00 across its 12 live campaigns. It can claw back earnings for fake engagement ([streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/how-to-earn-money-vyro)). MrBeast pays his direct clippers $50 per 100K views ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/29/n3on-spending-millions-stream-clippers-tiktok-kick/amp/)), so one clip needs roughly 1M views to earn $1,000, and per-clip caps cut off the upside. Content Rewards rejects submissions that miss a brief detail or lack an up-front disclosure, that reuse one edit across campaigns, or that were posted more than 30 minutes before submission ([Content Rewards](https://contentrewards.com/articles/why-clips-get-rejected)). Our posting kit therefore needs a per-campaign compliance checklist.

Native revenue is a bonus at best. YouTube Shorts pays creators 45% of their pool allocation. Shorts without music send 100% of their revenue to the pool (50% with one track). Views of "unedited clips" and "reuploads of others' content" earn nothing ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)). At the estimated RPM, 10M views come to roughly $100–700 ([Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/youtube-shorts-rpm-2026-calculator)), so keep Shorts music-free and the transformation visible. Since March 2025, Pakistani Facebook creators must link bank and tax details from the US, UK, UAE or India ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/03/17/facebook-quietly-tightens-monetization-policy-in-pakistan/)), and Content Monetization is invite-only ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/2026/03/18/creator-fast-track)). TikTok Creator Rewards excludes Pakistan and requires original videos of at least one minute ([ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/creator-fund-countries/)).

The durable income is services. Clipping retainers run **$500–1,500 per month per client** ([ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/how-to-price-clipping-services.html)). Strong edits sell for $100–400 per clip ([Osdire](https://osdire.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-video-editor-in-2026)), and podcast packages of 20 Shorts reach $2.5–3K per month ([Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/outsource-video-clipping)). These are vendor figures, but they are an order of magnitude above campaign earnings per hour. Our pipeline already produces agency-grade output, including clips, covers, a copy kit and loudness compliance. One retainer equals roughly 500K–1M campaign views a month. The risks are concrete. A ban forfeits all pending, unverified earnings on that account, and the main trigger is "content that looks the same post after post" ([OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/clipping-account-bans)). Bought engagement brings permanent marketplace bans. Undisclosed paid clips get hidden from main feeds on TikTok and Instagram ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/clipping-tests-limits-social-media-authenticity)). The realistic range from vendor data is $100–500 per month for beginners and $1–3K for consistent clippers posting 5–15 clips a day ([OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make)). No independent earnings distribution exists, so treat these as optimistic.

## Conclusion

"The Best Part" competes on judgement and finish, and AI tools are weakest exactly there. Our pipeline does not need OpusClip's automation. It needs the second half of OpusClip's business: fast candidate scoring for volume, plus copy and compliance packaging for six platforms and many briefs. The binding constraint is not editing quality but **payout rails and permissions from Pakistan**. Verify the rails before scaling volume, and treat every polished clip as a portfolio piece that sells a retainer, not just as views at $1 per thousand. Two numbers stay unknown until we measure them: whether Whop and Vyro pay out to Pakistan, and whether the LLM rubric predicts our real views better than our own picks. Settle both in the first month before investing further.
