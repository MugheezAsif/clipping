# Copyright, Content ID and blocked clips (research, 2026-11-19)

## Takeaway
No edit makes another creator's footage "safe". Content ID matches the footage itself, and fair use is a defence argued after a claim, not a shield at upload. The only reliable way to stop claims and blocks is a **license**: an active clipping campaign (Vyro, Whop Content Rewards) for that exact video, or written permission. Unlicensed clips should be treated as claim-expected and never fought with evasion tricks.

## Findings
- **How a Short gets blocked.** Content ID matches the footage or music, and the rights owner's policy decides: monetize (revenue goes to them), track, or block. Shorts over 1 minute with any active claim were blocked worldwide (policy since Oct 2024). Several 2026 guides report this auto-block was relaxed for new 1–3 min Shorts from 24 Sep 2026; unconfirmed, so we keep clips ≤58 s anyway. A block under 1 minute means the owner (or a music/rights agency) chose "block". — [YouTube Help: Manage Shorts as a rights holder](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317), [YouTube Help: Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en), [Exemplary](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-music-rules)
- **Claims are not strikes.** A claim or block gives no channel penalty. Strikes come from takedown requests; 3 in 90 days terminates a channel. Disputes are only valid with rights, a real exception such as fair use, or a mismatch. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en), [Trademark Engine](https://www.trademarkengine.com/blog/youtube-copyright-claims-vs-copyright-strikes-creators-need-to-know/)
- **MrBeast footage.** No public license to re-upload; unlicensed clips usually end in a Content ID claim. MrBeast licenses clipping through **Vyro** campaigns, and a campaign's permission covers only the videos it lists, not the whole channel. Budgets run out fast (one $1/1K round was used up in about a day). — [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/clip-mrbeast-videos-go-viral.html), [Clyper: MrBeast on Vyro](https://www.clyper.io/clipping-programs/mrbeast), [Vues: Vyro review](https://vues.app/blog/vyro-clipping-review)
- **Campaign licenses are narrow.** Content Rewards terms: a revocable license only for that campaign's clip; the clipper must clear any music they add; no reuse for another brand. — [Content Rewards](https://contentrewards.com/articles/is-clipping-legal)
- **Instagram/Facebook.** Since 30 Apr 2026 Instagram removes recommendations for accounts that mostly repost; "materially changed" edits (added elements that enhance the content) count as original, watermark or speed changes don't. Recovery: mostly original posts over a rolling 30 days. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator), [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- **Music.** Our SFX are CC0/CC BY. Third-party music inside the source can trigger separate claims; YouTube Studio's *Erase song* / *Trim* removes a claimed song. Licensed stock music can still be claimed if its owner uses Content ID. — [Foxi](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/youtube-shorts-copyright-music-library-guide/)

## Gaps
- No official source on whether Vyro-approved clips are allowlisted in MrBeast's Content ID; ask the campaign before posting.
- The 24 Sep 2026 change to the 1-minute rule is reported by guides only.
