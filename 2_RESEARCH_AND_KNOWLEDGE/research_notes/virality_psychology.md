# Psychology and Mechanics of Short-Form Video Virality (TikTok / Reels / Shorts, 2024-2026)

Evidence-grading key used throughout: **[PLATFORM]** = first-party platform statement/doc; **[ACADEMIC]** = peer-reviewed or academic preprint; **[INSIDER]** = leaked/internal production doc or named analyst with disclosed data; **[VENDOR]** = clipping/analytics tool vendor (commercial bias, usually proprietary or unsourced data); **[ANECDOTE]** = creator heuristic / unsourced. Older-than-2024 items are flagged **(pre-2024)**.

Note on research limits: ~17 tool calls; the Berger & Milkman PDF could not be text-extracted in-session (compressed PDF), so their numeric effect sizes below are flagged for verification.

## 1. Hook types and the first 1-3 seconds

### Takeaway
The only well-sourced quantitative platform evidence is that the opening seconds dominate swipe-away behavior and that top-performing TikTok creatives front-load the key message within 3 seconds; nearly all specific "3-second hold = X% more views" numbers are vendor- or single-creator data. For MrBeast-style clips, the strongest-evidenced pattern is: show the payoff-promise (stakes/spectacle) in frame 1, stated simply, with on-screen text as a second hook channel.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] TikTok's own ads creative guidance: over 63% of the highest-CTR videos highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds. Note this is a share of top-CTR videos, not a "63% CTR lift" (a common misreading on Bulk.ly and others). Ads context, not organic. — [TikTok Auction Ads Creative Tips (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf); misreading example: [Bulk.ly](https://bulk.ly/?p=48749)
- [PLATFORM] YouTube Studio exposes a Shorts metric "viewed vs. swiped away" ("How many chose to view") — the share of viewers who watched rather than swiped away. YouTube publishes no official benchmark or cutoff. — [YouTube Help: Understand your content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281); [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/); [Bytecap evidence synthesis (VENDOR, says no universal threshold exists)](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- [INSIDER] MrBeast's leaked "How to Succeed at MrBeast Production" (36 pages, leaked Sept 2024 via Rosanna Pansino; authenticity "allegedly" confirmed by her with four employees): the first minute is where most viewers leave and deserves outsized effort; the opening must prove the video delivers on the title/thumbnail; titles favor extreme framing ("I Survived" over "I Spent"); ideas must be graspable instantly ("the simpler the better"). Written for long-form, but directly transferable to the first 1-3 s of a clip. — [Alexander Jarvis hosted memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [NetInfluencer on the leak](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mrbeast-productions-leaked-internal-document-rosanna-pansino/); [Gigazine summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)
- [INSIDER] The leaked doc gives the example "We lost 21 million viewers in the first minute," noting that was still above platform average — i.e., heavy early drop-off is normal even at the top. — [Search summary of leak coverage; Sharp Daily](https://thesharpdaily.com/mrbeast-leaked-production-document-youtube-viral-success)
- [INSIDER, pre-2024 (2023)] Paddy Galloway & Chris Gileta study of ~5,400 Shorts from 33 channels: 50-60 s Shorts averaged ~1.7M views vs most creators making 20-40 s; Shorts with AVD >50 s averaged ~4.1M views (secondary summary); Galloway checked medians as well as means. A vidIQ secondary retelling says Shorts holding >=60% of viewers past the opening seconds cluster in the high-view region and that the hook is the main controllable lever. Note: this predates the March 2025 view-count change and the 3-minute Shorts limit. — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [videogen.io summary](https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views)
- [VENDOR, 2026] OverseerOS analysis of 4,784 hooks from 1M+ view videos (1,270 channels; 1,467 Shorts): 84.6% of English hooks were NOT questions; 32.5% used "you/your"; 14.2% contained a digit; 4,745 of 4,784 hook lines were unique; median hook length 12 words; most common Shorts opener "This" (~11.5%). Descriptive only — no failed-video control group, no retention data. Conclusion: no single formula dominates; copy mechanisms not lines. — [OverseerOS million-view hooks study](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/million-view-youtube-hooks-study)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] Frequently repeated but weakly sourced: "70% 3-second retention -> 8x more likely to hit 100K" (vendor's own clips); "70-85% 3-s retention -> 2.2x views" (unnamed "2025 industry data"); "65% who watch 3 s watch 10 s" (attributed to Meta internal data, unverified); "camera + subject movement in first second: 52% vs 23% retention" (single-author 1,000-video set). Treat as directional only. — [autoclip.dev](https://autoclip.dev/blog/viral-clip-anatomy-3-second-hook-data); [dev.to Hook Engineering](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9); [joyspace.ai](https://joyspace.ai/the-3-second-rule-science-stopping-scroll)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] On-screen text on frame 1 functions as a second hook channel alongside the spoken hook; large bold statements (claimed 40-60% of screen) on a blurred/dark background are read before the background image. MrBeast-style thumbnails/text use ~3-4 words max in huge high-contrast type. — [Blitzcut Shorts hooks 2026](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-youtube-shorts-hooks-2026); [OpusClip Shorts hook formulas](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas)
- [ACADEMIC, pre-2024] Ling et al. (arXiv 2021, 400 labeled TikToks): follower count was the strongest virality predictor; close-up and medium shot scales, presence of on-screen text, and point of view also mattered. — [arXiv 2111.02452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02452v1)

### Inferences
- Hook taxonomy for MrBeast-style clips (synthesized from the above; formulas are templates, not tested lines):
  - **Stakes/visual hook (strongest fit):** open on the most extreme frame (the $ pile, the drop, the fire, the face at the reveal) + 3-6 word overlay stating the stakes: "Last to leave wins $500,000"; "$1 vs $1,000,000 hotel"; "He has 10 seconds to decide."
  - **Curiosity-gap hook:** state an outcome without the mechanism: "He didn't expect what was inside"; "Only one of these is real."
  - **Contrarian/shock:** "This $10 phone beat a $1,000 one" (tech); "The cheapest one tasted best" (taste test).
  - **Bet/challenge framing:** "I bet him $10,000 he couldn't…" — a binary outcome the viewer can predict.
  - **Question hook:** usable, but the million-view dataset shows most winners are statements; use questions mainly when they invite a guess ("Which one is $1?").
  - **"Wait for it":** weakest-evidenced; better to show a flash of the payoff in frame 1 (a "flash-forward") then rewind, which delivers the TikTok "key message in 3 s" principle while keeping the gap open.
- The leaked doc's "deliver on the packaging" rule maps to Shorts: the first frame and overlay ARE the packaging, since there is no thumbnail click on the feed.

### Gaps
- No primary platform data quantifying how much 3-second hold rate moves distribution on TikTok, Reels or Shorts.
- No controlled study comparing visual vs text vs verbal hooks in short-form; all comparisons are vendor or anecdotal.
- Galloway's study is from 2023; I found no 2025-2026 replication by him.

## 2. Open loops, payoff timing, and mid-clip re-hooks

### Takeaway
Information-gap theory (Loewenstein) gives the mechanism: curiosity spikes when a specific, salient gap is opened with an implied promise to close it, and intensity follows an inverted U with uncertainty. The MrBeast doc operationalizes this as "re-engagement" beats and "stair-stepping" stakes with an abrupt ending after the payoff.

### Cited Findings
- [ACADEMIC, pre-2024 (1994 theory; 2021 extension)] Curiosity arises when attention is focused on a gap in knowledge; its intensity follows an inverted-U relative to uncertainty; making a question more salient increases curiosity for the answer (Golman, Loewenstein et al.). — [CMU: The demand for, and avoidance of, information](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/the-demand-for__and-avoidance-of__information.pdf); [arXiv clickbait/curiosity modeling](https://arxiv.org/html/1806.04212)
- [ACADEMIC] The gap is more intense when perceived as important and salient, and when there is an explicit or implicit promise of closing it; a 2022 study frames curiosity-gap YouTube titles in this framework. — [arXiv 1806.04212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.04212); [WSIZ journal 2022](https://journals.wsiz.edu.pl/sc/article/download/21/7_2022/30)
- [INSIDER] MrBeast doc structure: minutes 1-3 shift from hype to showing; "crazy progression" early; a "re-engagement" spectacle around minute 3 and again ~minute 6; quick scene changes; no dull moments; don't signal the ending early; "stair stepping" stakes toward the payoff; strong payoff and abrupt ending protect retention. — [Alexander Jarvis memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] MrBeast videos cover any waiting moment immediately with text overlays, reaction shots, images or SFX. — [Medium: 7 viral keys](https://medium.com/tech-titans/stop-blaming-the-budget-the-7-viral-keys-that-generate-mr-beasts-impossible-audience-retention-0a60469ecd7b)
- [ACADEMIC, preprint 2024] Li, Huai & Lin examine movie-commentary short videos and how plot narrative, emotional contagion and a "suggestive twist" affect liking and commenting intentions (not yet peer-reviewed per listing). — [OUCI listing](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4VOx8Pyl)

### Inferences
- Reusable clip structure (30-60 s), translating the long-form doc to short-form time scale:
  1. **0-2 s Hook:** stakes frame + overlay (opens Loop A: "will he win/what is it?").
  2. **2-8 s Context in one sentence:** rules of the challenge/bet, ideally overlay-only ("Last to take hand off car keeps it").
  3. **~8-15 s Escalation 1 (re-hook):** a twist or a raised stake ("…then we doubled it"). Opens Loop B before Loop A closes.
  4. **Mid-point (~40-60% of runtime) re-hook:** a reversal, failure, or a new contender; overlay like "But then…" / "He had one chance left."
  5. **Payoff:** close Loop A late (final 15-20%); keep the payoff short.
  6. **Abrupt end:** cut within ~1 s of the payoff reaction (doc: abrupt endings protect retention), which also feeds looping (section 4).
- Category templates: **Taste test** — "Rank them before the reveal" (gap = which is cheapest); **Tech** — "$X vs $Y" with the verdict withheld to the end; **Emotional reveal** — show the reaction face first, then rewind to "two weeks earlier"; **Danger stunt** — countdown/timer overlay as a visible open loop; **Bet** — state the odds and the consequence up front.
- Inverted-U implication: gaps that are too vague ("you won't believe this") or too obvious underperform; specific, concrete gaps ("Only 1 of 100 people can last 24 hours here") sit at the peak.

### Gaps
- No platform data on optimal payoff timing within short clips; no quantified evidence on mid-point re-hooks in Shorts specifically.
- No study testing forward-referencing ("what happened next") headlines/overlays directly.

## 3. Emotional triggers that drive shares

### Takeaway
The strongest academic evidence (Berger & Milkman, 2012) says high-arousal emotions — awe, anger, anxiety (and amusement/excitement in follow-ups) — drive sharing, while low-arousal sadness suppresses it; positive content is shared more than negative overall. This is pre-2024, text-article data, so it transfers to short video by analogy, but platform signals (Instagram "sends") now reward exactly this share behavior.

### Cited Findings
- [ACADEMIC, pre-2024 (2012)] Berger & Milkman, Journal of Marketing Research: analysis of ~7,000 New York Times articles' presence on the most-emailed list. Content evoking high-arousal positive (awe) or negative (anger, anxiety) emotions was more viral; low-arousal/deactivating emotions (sadness) less; effects held controlling for surprise, interest and practical value; positive content more viral than negative overall. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [Scientific American coverage](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral/); [Ragan: positive content most likely to go viral](https://www.ragan.com/study-positive-content-most-likely-to-go-viral/)
  - Commonly reported effect sizes (one SD increase -> odds of most-emailed: awe ~+30%, anger ~+34%, anxiety ~+21%, sadness ~-16%) — **could not verify from PDF text in this session; verify before quoting.**
- [ACADEMIC/book, pre-2024 (2013)] Berger's STEPPS (Contagious): Social currency (sharing makes the sharer look in-the-know), Triggers, Emotion ("When we care, we share"), Public, Practical value, Stories. Summaries report sad content ~20% less likely to be shared (secondary). — [Shortform STEPPS](https://www.shortform.com/blog/stepps-marketing/); [Promax talk summary](https://oldbrief.promax.org/article/the-conference-2014-jonah-berger-asks-and-answers-why-things-catch-on)
- [VENDOR] Semrush analysis: popular TikToks most often elicited happiness (58%) and surprise (24%). — [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/?p=322541)
- [ACADEMIC review, 2025] Literature review (Zenodo, not empirical) restates high-emotion content as more viral, naming awe, anger and nostalgia. — [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/17131364)
- [PLATFORM] Instagram head Adam Mosseri (Jan 2025) named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top ranking signals; sends "slightly more important" for unconnected (non-follower) reach. Claims that sends are "the single most important" or "3-5x likes" are third-party. — [Buffer Instagram algorithm guide](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/)

### Inferences
- Mapping MrBeast-style clip types to share emotions: **awe** (scale, money, danger stunts, extreme tech); **inspiration/elevation** (giving, emotional reveals — positive high arousal; keep it uplifting rather than sad to avoid the low-arousal penalty); **amusement** (taste-test reactions, failed bets); **anxiety/suspense** (stunts, timers); **outrage** — highest risk and mostly off-brand; use mild "that's unfair" tension rather than anger bait.
- Social currency is the main "send" driver: overlays that make the sharer the messenger ("Send this to the friend who'd last 1 hour"; "You won't believe this is legal") — but see section 5 on bait limits.
- For emotional reveals, cut the sad build-up short and lead with the joyful reaction to keep the clip in high-arousal positive territory.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2024-2026 study of emotion -> share rate on TikTok/Reels/Shorts found; most short-video emotion data is vendor-produced.

## 4. Rewatch and loop engineering

### Takeaway
Loops are mechanically meaningful on YouTube since 31 March 2025, because every start or replay now counts as a public view (monetization still uses "engaged views"); TikTok reports "Rewatched" in analytics. But no platform documents how heavily rewatches weigh in ranking, and all quantified loop lift claims are vendor anecdotes.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] From March 31, 2025, YouTube counts a Shorts view each time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time; the old metric is "engaged views" (Advanced Mode), which still governs YPP eligibility and revenue. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/)
- [PLATFORM-adjacent] TikTok analytics expose "Rewatched": how many times viewers watched again after it finished without swiping. — [Lilach Bullock explainer](https://www.lilachbullock.com/what-does-rewatched-mean-on-tiktok-analytics/)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] Loop technique: final frame nearly matches opening frame plus audio that repeats cleanly so the restart is seamless; shorter clips (claimed 20-25 s highest completion, Zebracat 2025 data) loop better; "30% higher completion" and "rewatches weighted 2x" claims are unsourced. — [virvid.ai looping structure](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026); [Medium looping](https://medium.com/@henryhienton/how-to-hack-the-tiktok-algorithm-to-improve-completion-retention-rate-with-looping-videos-b72e466412d5); [crayo.ai](https://crayo.ai/blog/how-to-make-looping-content-for-tiktok)
- [INSIDER] MrBeast doc: abrupt endings after the payoff protect retention. — [Alexander Jarvis memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- Why fast/ambiguous endings drive rewatches (mechanism inferred, not proven): (a) a payoff that happens in <1 s ("did he actually catch it?") leaves residual uncertainty, prompting re-viewing to verify; (b) if the clip ends mid-sentence that completes the opening line ("…and that's why" -> loop to "He spent $1M on…"), autoplay restart reads as continuous; (c) a hidden detail called out in the overlay ("Watch his hand at 0:03") invites deliberate rewatch.
- Loop recipes: **sentence loop** (end line completes the first line); **visual match** (end on same framing as frame 1); **reveal-then-rewind** (frame 1 is the result, ending returns to frame 1).
- Since a YouTube replay now counts as a view but revenue uses engaged views, looping inflates public counts more than earnings — useful for social proof, not a monetization hack.

### Gaps
- No platform statement on the ranking weight of loops/rewatches; no independent study of ambiguity -> rewatch.

## 5. Comment bait techniques and ethical/platform limits

### Takeaway
Genuine opinion prompts (debate, "which would you pick", prediction) are a legitimate engagement lever, but explicit, mechanical asks ("comment YES", "like if…") and deceptive tactics are explicitly penalized: TikTok makes manipulative engagement-seeking content ineligible for the For You feed, and Meta has demoted "engagement bait" since 2017.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] TikTok Community Guidelines (Integrity & Authenticity): content is ineligible for the For You feed if it seeks to trick or manipulate others to increase gifts, followers, likes, views or other engagement metrics. — [TikTok Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/integrity-authenticity/)
- [PLATFORM, pre-2024 (2017)] Meta/Facebook demotes engagement bait in five categories: vote baiting, react baiting, share baiting, tag baiting, comment baiting (e.g., "comment YES if you love rock"); repeat offenders' Pages see larger reach drops; posts genuinely asking for help/advice/recommendations are exempt. Current status not re-verified. — [Meta Newsroom: Fighting Engagement Bait](https://about.fb.com/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/)
- [ACADEMIC] Loewenstein-coauthored work found clickbait/viral incentives reduced informational content of summaries — a reminder that curiosity optimization can degrade substance. — [CMU demand/avoidance paper](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/demandavoidance.pdf)
- [INSIDER] MrBeast doc stresses that packaging must match content; mismatch causes viewers to leave. — [Alexander Jarvis memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- Safer comment drivers (natural debate, no explicit mechanical ask): "Which would you pick: $100K now or the mystery box?"; "Would you last longer than 4 hours?"; "Rank these 1-5" in a taste test; deliberately leaving the winner's choice undisclosed until the final frame; showing a contestable judgment ("He chose wrong").
- Polarizing framing is effective but has a ceiling: mild contrarian takes are fine; misleading claims (fake outcomes, fake prizes, fake scarcity) cross into TikTok's "trick or manipulate" language and also violate the packaging-match rule that protects retention.
- Ethical line for clippers: never imply an outcome the source footage does not contain; don't caption a stunt as more dangerous than it was; avoid outrage at real private people.

### Gaps
- No public data quantifying how much question overlays raise comment rate; no current (2025-2026) Meta statement on engagement-bait enforcement for Reels found.

## 6. Share/save triggers; on-screen questions; titles, captions and hashtags (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Instagram explicitly weights sends per reach (esp. for non-follower reach); on TikTok, captions, on-screen text and spoken words are treated (per industry consensus, not TikTok docs) as search/indexing signals, with hashtags now low-weight. Shares come from social currency and practical value; saves from utility.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] Mosseri (Jan 2025): sends per reach is among the most important ranking signals; out of everyone who saw it, how many sent it in a DM. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-insights-into-its-algorithms-creator-monetization/718540/); [Uppbeat: carousels and sends](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/whats-working-on-instagram-right-now-carousels-and-sends-says-adam-mosseri)
- [VENDOR/industry consensus] TikTok search reads captions, hashtags, on-screen text (burned-in captions/overlays), spoken audio (auto-transcript) and possibly file names; keywords work best when consistent across caption, overlay and speech; hashtags carry modest weight ("#foryou guarantees nothing"). Not confirmed by TikTok docs. — [Hootsuite TikTok SEO](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-seo/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/?p=175466); [OpenClip metadata](https://openclip.app/blog/metadata-impacts-tiktok-seo)
- [ACADEMIC, pre-2024] Practical value and social currency are two of the six STEPPS drivers of sharing. — [Shortform STEPPS](https://www.shortform.com/blog/stepps-marketing/)

### Inferences
- For MrBeast-style clips: put the searchable noun phrase in both the overlay and caption ("$1 vs $100,000 hotel", "last to leave", "iPhone vs Android drop test"); hashtags 2-4 specific ones at most.
- Share triggers: "send to" prompts tied to a specific person-type (social currency); awe moments; surprising facts. Save triggers: rankings, "which is worth it" tech comparisons (practical value).

### Gaps
- No TikTok or YouTube first-party document quantifying caption/hashtag weight in 2025-2026; no source on whether YouTube Shorts titles drive Shorts-feed distribution vs. search only.

## 7. Retention curve shapes and benchmarks

### Takeaway
No platform publishes benchmarks; creator-reported heuristics converge on ~70-80% "viewed" (vs swiped) on Shorts as strong and 3-second hold of ~70% as a target, but these are anecdotal. The defensible guidance is channel-relative comparison by length cohort and diagnosing curve shape.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] YouTube offers a frame-level Shorts retention curve and "viewed vs. swiped away"; no official benchmark. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] Viewed-share targets: 75-80% viewed; ">70% viewed = strong hook"; ">=80% top tier" — all explicitly creator-reported. An example channel showed a 90-day split of 26.6% viewed / 73.4% swiped, illustrating wide variation. Benchmarks vary by topic, length, traffic mix, familiarity, language. — [Subscribr Shorts analytics](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-viral); [gofaceless benchmarks](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks); [1of10 Shorts analytics](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- [VENDOR, 2026] Bytecap: no universal good-retention threshold exists; compare by length cohorts (15-30 s, 30-60 s, longer); strong opening + sharp mid-drop needs a different fix than a weak first frame with stable completion. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- [INSIDER, pre-2024] Galloway: Shorts keeping >=60% past opening seconds cluster with high-view outcomes; AVD >50 s associated with ~4.1M avg views (secondary). — [videogen.io summary](https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views)
- [VENDOR] Loop-driven clips can show >100% average viewed (one client test reported 115%). — [virvid.ai](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026)

### Inferences
- Curve shapes to diagnose: **cliff at 0-3 s** = hook/packaging failure; **steady slope with mid-dip** = missing re-hook at the dip; **flat then drop near end** = payoff telegraphed or ending too long; **curve >100% / bump at end** = loop/rewatch working.
- Working targets (heuristic, label as such): viewed vs swiped >=70%; for <30 s clips average % viewed near or above 100% (looping); for 45-60 s clips aim for >=60-70% average viewed.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) 2024-2026 dataset of average % viewed by length for Shorts/TikTok/Reels; OpusClip/TubeBuddy/vidIQ retention studies were not located with transparent methodology in this pass.
