# Short-Form Platform Algorithms (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts), 2025-2026

Research date: 2026-11-17. Every claim carries a date. Source quality: official help pages (YouTube Help) and established tech press (Engadget, TechCrunch, Tubefilter, 9to5Google) are primary or near-primary. Many results were third-party marketing blogs, and they are flagged as such. Several primary pages could not be fetched: PetaPixel returned 403, and TikTok's FYF page did not include the unoriginal-content section.

## 1. Ranking signals per platform (with official statements and dates)

### Takeaway
Instagram has named its top Reels signals publicly: watch time, likes per reach, and sends (DM shares) per reach. Mosseri said this in January/February 2025 and reaffirmed it through 2026. YouTube's public metric is "viewed vs. swiped away" ("chose to view"), and engaged views remain the monetization and analytics metric after two view-count changes (March 31, 2025 and August 24, 2026). TikTok publishes no signal weights. Watch time and completion are consistently reported as the heaviest signals, but only by third-party sources.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram Reels**
- In January 2025, Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach as the three most important Reels ranking factors. This is reported by multiple secondary sources, and the original post was not retrieved. — [SocialPilot (Sept 2026)](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm); German coverage dates the statement to February 2025 ("Watchtime, Like Rate und Send Rate") — [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-reels-metriken-adam-mosseri-insights)
- Mosseri recommends judging Trial Reels by likes per reach and sends per reach as ratios. In a separate post he cited watch time, likes, and sends as key ranking metrics (2025). — [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [Instagram @creators blog on Trial Reels](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)
- Trial Reels (launched December 2024) show a Reel to non-followers only. In 2025 they were expanded to all public accounts with at least 1,000 followers, and scheduling was added. Instagram reported in June 2025 that Trial Reels drove an 80% increase in non-follower Reels reach, and that 40% of users posted more often. These are Instagram's own figures. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-introduces-wider-access-trial-reels-9493132); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagram-will-let-video-creators-make-trial-reels-for-non-followers-160016725.html); [Tubefilter Jan 23, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/23/instagram-trial-reels-growth-hacking-creator-economy/)
- A ranking explainer by Mosseri reportedly lists four broader priorities: originality, recency, breaking content, and model size. Mosseri's December 31, 2025 year-end memo reportedly says Instagram will prioritize "raw, real human content" over AI-generated material in 2026 (secondary). — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- Mosseri's September 2026 comments defended the existing Reels ranking rather than announcing a new one (secondary). — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm)
- Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to non-followers (Buffer, November 12, 2026). — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length)

**YouTube Shorts**
- On March 31, 2025, YouTube changed Shorts so that a public view counts whenever a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The older metric was kept as "engaged views" in YouTube Studio. YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue share still use engaged views. The change was announced March 26, 2025. — [Tubefilter Mar 26, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-metric/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-view-count-update/)
- From August 24, 2026, public views on all formats (long-form, live, podcasts, Shorts) count from the first frame. Engaged views ("viewer kept watching past the initial start") remain in Studio. Earnings and YPP are unaffected, and views on earlier uploads keep their old totals. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-view-count-update/)
- YouTube Studio's Shorts analytics show "viewed vs. swiped away" ("chose to view"): the share of feed impressions where the viewer kept watching instead of swiping. YouTube has not stated that this exact number is a ranking input. Blogs describe Shorts as testing each video on a seed audience and expanding or contracting reach from its response. — [Buffer (citing YouTube)](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [vidIQ 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- The most detailed official Shorts algorithm explainer remains the Creator Insider / Todd Sherman interview from August 2023. I found no verifiable 2025-2026 replacement. — [TechCrunch Aug 25, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)

**TikTok**
- Third-party explainers (2025-2026) consistently rank completion rate and total watch time as the heaviest inputs, followed by shares, then rewatches, saves, comments, and negative feedback ("Not interested"). TikTok publishes no weights, so any percentage online is a guess. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/); [Webtonic 2026](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/tiktok-algorithm); [viral.app](https://viral.app/en/grow/tiktok-algorithm-explained)
- TikTok's official FYF page says it makes some content "not suitable for a broad audience" ineligible for For You. It also says it avoids repeatedly recommending certain categories (extreme fitness/dieting, sexual suggestiveness, sadness, overgeneralized mental-health advice). — [TikTok Community Guidelines FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)

### Inferences
- For clippers, the cross-platform constant is retention in the first 1-3 seconds. That moment decides swipe vs. view on Shorts and is the main lever on watch time on TikTok and Reels. The second lever is "shareability": sends per reach on Instagram and shares on TikTok.
- Because public Shorts views now count from the first frame (since 2025/2026), engaged views and "viewed vs. swiped" are the honest KPIs for comparing clip performance.

### Gaps
- No primary Mosseri post text was retrieved for the January/February 2025 "sends" statement, and the January vs. February date differs between sources.
- No official TikTok document lists ranking signals for 2025-2026, so the signal order above comes from third parties.
- No official YouTube confirmation that "viewed vs. swiped away" feeds ranking.

## 2. Ideal and maximum clip lengths (as of 2026)

### Takeaway
Maximum lengths: YouTube Shorts 3 min (since October 15, 2024). Reels can be uploaded up to 20 min (rolled out late 2025), but only Reels of 3 min or less are recommended to non-followers. TikTok allows 10 min in general, with 60-minute uploads tested since 2024 and reportedly expanded in 2025. For reach, Buffer's 2026 data on 2.7M Reels favors 90-179 s, but differences under 90 s are small. Over 3 minutes is a cliff on Instagram.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube: since October 15, 2024, new vertical or square videos of 1-3 minutes are classified as Shorts. Any Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim (including manual claims) is **blocked globally** until the claim is resolved. Shorts audio-library songs can be used only in Shorts up to 1 minute. For longer Shorts, use the YouTube Audio Library. Official Artist Channels have a separate cutoff date of December 8, 2025. — [YouTube Help: Understand three-minute Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- Instagram: Reels can be up to 20 minutes. The limit was raised from 3 minutes in late 2025, and not every account has it yet (third-party, not confirmed from Instagram Help). — [SocialCal 2026](https://www.socialcal.app/blog/instagram-video-length-limits-2026); [Buffer Nov 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length)
- Buffer study (published November 12, 2026; 2,730,166 Reels posted January 1 to August 15, 2026; reach measured against each account's own baseline): 90-179 s +7.4% reach; 60-89 s +1.6%; 30-59 s +2.7%; 15-29 s −0.9%; under 15 s −1.5%; 180 s or longer −49.3% reach and −45.9% views, but +2.65 percentage points engagement rate. Every bucket under 90 s fell within about 3% of baseline. For most accounts, the 90 s to 3 min lift was about 4-7%. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length)
- A separate third-party analysis found 45-60 s Reels had the highest engagement rate (0.35%), versus 0.15% for Reels over 3 minutes. — [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
- TikTok: in May 2024 TikTok confirmed to TechCrunch that it was testing 60-minute uploads with select users. The general limit was then 10 minutes, with 15 for some creators. Online Marketing (April 2025) reports that more users can upload 60-minute videos. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/tiktok-testing-60-minute-video-uploads-440588); [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/tiktok-longform-update-60-minuten-videos-rollout)
- Third-party TikTok spec guides for 2026 conflict. One lists a 10-minute organic limit, and another says 10 min for verified accounts and 3 min by default for newer accounts. — [Heyorca 2026](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/tiktok-media-specs-best-practices-2026); [PostFast](https://postfa.st/sizes/tiktok/video)
- TikTok Creator Rewards requires videos of at least 1 minute (this is a monetization eligibility rule, not an upload limit; secondary mention). — [TikTok algorithm search results, e.g. viral.app](https://viral.app/en/grow/tiktok-algorithm-explained)

### Inferences
- For clips cut from other creators' YouTube videos, keep Shorts **at or under 60 s** whenever the source might be Content ID-protected. A claim on a 61 s+ Short blocks it worldwide, while a claim on a Short of 60 s or less normally just routes revenue to the claimant.
- On Reels, aim for 30-90 s by default and test 90-179 s for story-driven clips. Never exceed 3:00 if non-follower reach is the goal.
- On TikTok, clips of 60 s or longer are needed for Creator Rewards eligibility. Shorter clips can still win on completion rate.

### Gaps
- No official TikTok Help page confirming the current universal upload maximum (10 vs. 60 min) in 2026.
- No platform-official "ideal length" data. All optimal-length figures come from third parties (Buffer, Socialinsider).

## 3. Originality, unoriginal and reused content policies

### Takeaway
All three platforms penalize unedited reposts. Instagram (Reels from April 30, 2024, extended to photos and carousels in April 2026) swaps in the original, labels the repost, and removes repeat aggregators from recommendations to non-followers. YouTube's reused-content policy (unchanged by the July 15, 2025 "inauthentic content" rename) demonetizes clips without meaningful commentary or editing at the channel level. TikTok (guidelines updated August 2025, effective about September 13, 2025) makes unoriginal or reposted content without added creative edits ineligible for the For You feed. The transformations that count are voiceover or commentary, reaction with voice, a new narrative, and substantial audio/visual edits. Borders, text overlays, and light edits are not reliably enough.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram**
- From April 30, 2024: when two identical pieces of content are detected, the original replaces the repost in recommendations. Reposts get a label crediting the original creator. Aggregators that posted unoriginal content **10 or more times in the previous 30 days** stop being recommended. Exemptions cover "significant" changes such as voice-overs, reaction clips, or posts "materially edited to become a meme". This initially applied to Reels only. — [Engadget Apr 30, 2024](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- From around April 30, 2026, Mosseri extended the repost crackdown beyond Reels to photos and carousels. Accounts that mainly repost become ineligible for recommendations across Instagram. Eligibility returns **30 days after the last unoriginal repost**. Significantly changed content is not replaced. Publishers with licensing agreements or explicit permission are exempt. The platform assesses an account's output over a month. — [PetaPixel Apr 30, 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/) (page returned 403, details taken from search snippets); [MediaPost](https://mediapost.com/publications/article/414773/instagram-algorithm-update-discourages-reposted-co.html); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/instagrams-new-ranking-system-prioritizes-original-content)

**YouTube (YPP monetization, applies at channel level)**
- Reused content means repurposing existing material "without meaningful original commentary, substantive changes, or added educational or entertainment value". Permission from the original creator does **not** exempt content, and copyright is a separate matter. — [YouTube Help: YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Allowed: clips used in critical review; reactions that comment on the original; edited footage with added storyline and commentary; sports replays explaining moves; movie scenes with rewritten dialogue and new voiceover; content where the creator is visible or explains their contribution; edits with substantial audio/visual work that make the video distinct. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Not allowed: clips edited together with little or no narrative (compilations); short videos compiled from other social sites; reactions that rely mainly on non-verbal reaction without voice commentary; content downloaded or copied with no substantive changes; promoting others' content even with permission; reading others' text. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- On July 15, 2025, "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" (mass-produced or repetitive content, templated storylines, slideshows or scrolling text with minimal value, generic templated AI content). The reused-content policy was **not** changed. YouTube's Rene Ritchie said the update targets spam, not reaction or clip channels. Monetization may be removed from the whole channel. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [PlagiarismToday Jul 8, 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)

**TikTok**
- A Community Guidelines update published around August 14-15, 2025 took effect around September 13, 2025. Unoriginal or repeatedly reused content without added value is excluded from the For You feed. The earlier wording covered content imported from other platforms or sources such as TV and film "where the user has not added new, creative edits". The guidelines do not define "added value". — [TechCrunch Aug 15, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/); [blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/sk/vedomosti/social-media/algoritmy-socialnych-sieti-a-distribucia/tiktok-algoritmus-for-you-page)
- Third-party guides report that light edits or overlays may not avoid matching, and that flagged posts show the reason in analytics and can be appealed (unofficial). — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/tiktok-slideshow-unoriginal-content-flag)

### Inferences
- A safe transformation recipe that works across all three platforms: an on-camera presence or voiceover commentary, a re-narrated structure (hook, context, payoff), captions plus motion graphics and B-roll, and a recut timeline. Avoid raw downloads with only a caption bar or border.
- Clipping programs (licensed or permissioned clipping) are exempt on Instagram (2026) but **not** on YouTube's monetization policy. Permission does not satisfy YouTube's reused-content rule.
- A clipping account posting many minimally edited reposts risks account-level throttling on Instagram (30-day window) and channel-level demonetization on YouTube.

### Gaps
- No platform quantifies "significant" or "substantive" change, for example whether captions plus zoom cuts alone qualify.
- TikTok's exact current FYF-ineligibility wording could not be fetched from TikTok directly.

## 4. Technical specs (resolution, bitrate, frame rate, loudness, safe zones)

### Takeaway
Use 1080x1920 (9:16), H.264 MP4 with AAC audio, at 30 fps (or the source fps up to 60), around -14 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP. Keep text out of the top ~14%, the bottom ~35%, and ~6% at each side, plus the right-side button rail. Almost all spec figures come from third-party guides, and official organic-video specs are thin.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok: 9:16 at 1080x1920 recommended, minimum ~540x960, MP4/H.264 + AAC 44.1 kHz. Recommended bitrates vary (4-8 Mbps or 5-10 Mbps for 1080p). The only firm figure is the ad minimum of 516 kbps. File size limits differ by method: about 287.6 MB on mobile and up to 4 GB on the web uploader. TikTok publishes safe-zone templates on its ad specs page. — [Heyorca 2026](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/tiktok-media-specs-best-practices-2026); [Sked Social](https://skedsocial.com/blog/tiktok-video-size-guide); [PostFast](https://postfa.st/sizes/tiktok/video)
- Meta (Reels, Instagram and Facebook) ad safe zone: keep the top 14%, the bottom 35%, and 6% on each side clear. On 1080x1920 that is about 269 px top, 672 px bottom, and 65 px per side. Reels with disclaimers need the bottom 40% clear. The bottom figure varies across sources (20-40%). — [AdManage Reels safe zone](https://admanage.ai/instagram-reels-safe-zone); [Adkit Meta safe zones](https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/meta)
- Loudness: YouTube (including Shorts) normalizes to about -14 LUFS, and -1 dBTP true peak is recommended. Sources conflict on TikTok and Instagram. Some estimate about -14 LUFS, some say they prefer louder mixes of -12 to -10 LUFS, and some say no normalization at all. No official spec was found. Practical consensus is -14 to -16 LUFS with -1 dBTP. — [Fora Soft Jun 5, 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026); [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization)
- Shorts: vertical or square videos up to 3 min are Shorts (since October 15, 2024). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A single 1080x1920 H.264 master at about -14 LUFS, with captions placed roughly 300-700 px above the bottom edge and kept clear of the right ~120 px rail, works on all three platforms.

### Gaps
- No official organic-upload bitrate or frame-rate spec from TikTok, Instagram, or YouTube Shorts was retrieved. No official YouTube Shorts safe-zone pixel figures were found. Loudness targets for TikTok and Instagram are unofficial.

## 5. Posting cadence, first-hour testing pools, trending audio

### Takeaway
All three platforms test new posts on a small seed audience before widening reach. Instagram now lets creators test explicitly on non-followers with Trial Reels. No official cadence numbers were found. Music limits matter on YouTube: library tracks are allowed only in Shorts up to 1 minute.

### Cited Findings
- Shorts and TikTok distribute in stages. A Short is shown to a small test group, then reach expands or stops based on retention and swipe-away (third-party description). — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-does-youtube-shorts-algorithm-work); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- Instagram Trial Reels test with non-followers first. Since 2025 they are available to public accounts with at least 1,000 followers, with scheduling. Some creators repost old high performers as Trial Reels. — [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [Tubefilter Jan 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/23/instagram-trial-reels-growth-hacking-creator-economy/)
- Mosseri (2024) suggested that reaching about 20% of a creator's followers is normal algorithm behavior. — [Engadget Apr 30, 2024](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- YouTube Shorts music: Shorts audio-library songs are allowed only in Shorts up to 1 minute. Longer Shorts should use YouTube Audio Library royalty-free music, which is not claimed. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Testing hooks with Trial Reels on Instagram before posting to followers is a low-risk way to A/B test clip openings.
- Trending audio is useful on TikTok and Reels. On Shorts longer than 60 s, licensed trending tracks risk blocking.

### Gaps
- No official or credible 2025-2026 data was found on optimal posting frequency or on a defined "first-hour" window for any platform. Claims about a "first hour" or "golden hour" are folklore unless sourced.
- No official statement quantifying the ranking boost from trending audio on TikTok or Reels in 2025-2026.
